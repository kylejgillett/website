Research

Research focus

How rear-flank storm interactions influence supercell behavior and tornadogenesis

Storm interactions are known to induce changes in parent supercell storm behavior, including the production of tornadoes (e.g., Lemon 1976; Lee et al. 2006; Hastings and Richardson 2014; Fischer and Dahl 2023; Nixon et al. 2024; Werkema et al. 2025). Why this is true remains a question often asked but generally unanswered. To address this, idealized numerical simulations are used to investigate the full three-dimensional evolution of rear-flank storm interactions (which are often associated with increased tornado production, e.g., Nixon et al. 2024). Changes in storm behavior and tornado-like vortex production are tracked and physical mechanisms governing interaction outcome are investigated.

Simulated parent supercells subjected to rear-flank storm interactions exhibited dramatic increases in tornado-like-vortex (TLV) production. In one simulation, the parent supercell's accumulated TLV duration was 35 minutes longer than the control simulation. Rear-flank downdraft (RFD) behavior also changed markedly, becoming stronger and facilitating a state of inflow-outflow balance beneath the parent's low-level updraft. These changes in RFD behavior, perhaps the most important influence of the storm interaction, appear to be driven in part by both buoyancy and dynamic mechanisms.

Among the most interesting aspects of the simulated storm interactions was the fact that storm behavior appeared to change before the interaction appeared to "begin" on low-altitude simulated reflectivity. Investigating this further revealed the obvious: the storm interaction was also taking place aloft and doing so before it appeared at lower altitudes (also observed by Byers and Braham 1949, Wescott and Kennedy 1989, and Lee et al. 2006).

The simulated storm interactions start aloft as much as 15–20 minutes before appearing in low-altitude reflectivity. Composite reflectivity may serve as a tool to help identify interactions occurring aloft and perhaps increase the lead time for anticipating interaction outcomes. The interaction starting aloft also played a major role in perturbing parent storm behavior through hydrometeor seeding and several dynamic mechanisms.

Master's Thesis | University of North Dakota

Effects of a Rear-Flank Storm Interaction on Supercell Behavior and Tornado-like Vortex Development in Idealized Simulations - A Master's Thesis presented to the University of North Dakota Department of Atmospheric Science; July 2026

Committee: Dr. Catherine Finley, Dr. Bruce D. Lee, Dr. Cameron J. Nixon with special thanks to Dr. Matthew Brown, Dr. Jordan Christian, Dr. Jacob Carstens, Brandon Black, & Chris Broyles

Thesis Abstract

Observationally, storm interactions have been associated with changes in severe convective storm behavior and evolution, including the development and maintenance of tornadoes. In particular, the interaction of neighboring convection in or near the rear flank of a supercell thunderstorm has frequently been associated with tornadogenesis. Although statistical relationships for these changes in behavior have been established, the underlying physical mechanisms governing their evolution remain poorly understood, owing in part to limited spatial and temporal resolution of most observational datasets. Previous modeling efforts also lack the design and resolution required to sufficiently examine this problem. This study aims to complement and build upon existing findings by investigating the impact of auxiliary convection near the rear flank of a weakly tornadic supercell thunderstorm. Presented are two sets of numerical experiments, each consisting of five idealized model simulations. One experiment set simulates a single discrete "parent" supercell thunderstorm, while the other simulates the same "parent" subjected to a rear-flank interaction with an auxiliary "neighbor" cell. The interaction simulations reveal drastic changes in overall storm behavior and production of tornado-like vortices. The results suggest both buoyant and dynamic contributions to these changes and provide further evidence that rear-flank storm interactions can modulate overall storm behavior, which in turn modulates tornado development and maintenance.

Research focus

Can tropical cyclone tornadoes be forecast before landfall using airborne observations?

Convective Environments Preceding Landfall in Tornadic Tropical Cyclones: An Analysis of Airborne Observations. Presented at the 106th AMS Annual Meeting in Houston, Texas

Advisor: Dr. Jacob D. Carstens

Synopsis: An analysis of ~30,000 dropsondes deployed from recon aircraft between 1995-2021 (Nguyen et al. 2019) are sorted by storm and then into two categories: weakly/non-tornadic Tropical Cyclones (TCs) and highly tornadic TCs using the Tropical Cyclone Tornado database (TCTOR, Edwards, 2010). Weakly/non-tornadic (highly-tornadic) TCs are defined as TCs producing <15 (>=15) tornadoes. Individual dropsonde points for both subsets were then spatially organized into 50 x 50 km bins relative to TC-center. After quality controlling the data (removing points beyond standard deviation criteria, interpolating over missing data, etc.), mean vertical profiles were constructed for each bin, revealing a 50 x 50 km area-averaged vertical profile in each bin for both subsets. A few relevant convective storm environment parameters and vertical profiles were compared to evaluate the differences between the two subsets. Preliminary takeaways from this work suggest that highly tornadic TCs exhibit "more favorable" thermodynamic and kinematic environmental configurations in their right-front storm-relative quadrant than weakly/non-tornadic TCs. Full project abstract

SounderPy is a simple, open-source Python package for retrieving and plotting vertical profile (sounding) data. Built for simplicity and reliability for all uses and users, this project’s goal is to provide a uniform method for sounding analysis across multiple data types. Severe weather analysis and forecasting requires a sound comprehension of thermodynamic and kinematic properties of the environment. SounderPy makes this possible with robust access to data and custom visualizations. The tool creates complex yet effective sounding and hodograph plots with high readability which are designed specifically for severe weather analysis and forecasting. SounderPy is capable of retrieving and plotting model forecast data, observed radiosonde data, Aircraft Communications Addressing and Reporting System (ACARS) observation data, and model reanalysis data. All of this functionality can be completed in three simple lines of code or less, making SounderPy an accessible tool for both Python experts and novices. A number of scientific Python libraries build the base of SounderPy’s efficient and durable functionality, such as NumPy, Matplotlib, xarray, MetPy, and SHARPpy. SounderPy is available through GitHub and PyPI and is distributed under an MIT license.
